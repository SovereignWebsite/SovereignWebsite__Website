Owning your domain

Your address, in your own name.

A website you own includes its name. The registrar is where the name is really held, so it matters which one you pick: who it answers to, what the second year costs, and what can be done with the account if a key is ever handed to an agent.

Why it decides your level

A site that is portable in its code but registered in someone else's name is not sovereign. On the maturity model, a site cannot reach level 4, portable, until its domain is registered to its owner, at a registrar the owner chose.

On a hosted sovereign website, the site has its own address on sovereignwebsite.site until you point your own domain at it. You do not need a domain to start, and the one you bring stays yours.

What to check before you register

  • It is in your name. Your own account, at a registrar you chose, not ours or an agency's.
  • The renewal price, not only the first year. First-year prices often double or triple. At Gandi a .com was €11.65 the first year and €31.98 after.
  • Whether privacy is free. Some registrars include it; INWX and Infomaniak charge for it.
  • The rules of your extension. A .ai name needs a two-year minimum everywhere. A .eu name needs an owner in the EU or EEA.
  • What a key can do. A registrar's API can buy, transfer and repoint names. Prefer one that lets you narrow a key to one domain, take away purchasing and cap spending, before any agent is given one.
  • Strong sign-in, and that it survives a move. In July 2024, domains moved from Google Domains to Squarespace were hijacked because multi-factor authentication was not carried over.

Source: Your own registrar and Static against the platforms.

Three put forward for a European small business

From sixteen registrars compared on their own pages on 19 November 2026, these three were put forward, each with its catch.

  • OVHcloud

    France

    An EU company with low, steady prices on European names (a .eu €7.49 then €8.59, a .pt €16.99 then €13.39, excluding VAT), DNSSEC included, and an official MCP server for agents that cannot pay.

    Catch: a .com renews at €14.29 after €8.49, and a .ai at €119.99 after €70.99; whether privacy is free could not be confirmed.

  • Hostinger

    Lithuania

    EU-based, with .com, .eu, .pt, .co.uk and .ai, free privacy, a public API and an official MCP server with an OAuth option for agents.

    Catch: promotional first years; the $0.01 .com needs a three-year term and renews at $19.99, and the .ai more than doubles.

  • INWX

    Germany

    A registrar built around its API, with a free test environment and official Terraform and Ansible tools, and all five of those extensions (a .ai at €100 for two years).

    Catch: privacy is a paid extra, and its renewal prices would not load when compared.

Two others, for context. AWS now sells .ai, at $137 a year with privacy not supported, but not .pt, and a full AWS account is a lot to ask of a small business to hold one domain. Cloudflare sells at cost, with privacy and DNSSEC, but not .eu or .pt, and a domain registered there must use its own nameservers.

Prices are as each registrar's own pages showed on 19 November 2026, in the currency and tax basis shown; they change, and some were promotions. This is not paid placement: none of these registrars knows about it, and nothing was bought. The full comparison of sixteen is in Your own registrar.
