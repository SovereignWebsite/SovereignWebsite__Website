From a conversation to five designs

  1. An interview

    Most people do not know what they want from a website until someone asks them the right questions. An AI asks about what the site is for, the sections you want, what you have, three websites you like and why, and what to avoid.

  2. Your material

    A zip of photos, documents and videos, an email, or links to what is already public.

  3. A team of agents builds it

    A conductor, a researcher, a designer, a developer and a security reviewer, each with a written job description and a behaviour policy, each signing its work.

  4. Five designs

    Inspired by the sites you said you like: not copies, but the qualities you named, in five different readings. You pick one; the chosen one goes live and the others are kept.

  5. Hand-over

    The site is handed over to you, in a repository you control.

  6. Your first change

    You change your own site by talking to your AI. A sentence, a photo, a CV, and the site is different, checked and live.

The interview as the first step is a design that is still being built. Source: Start with an interview, Partners sell, we build and The sovereign website.

Then it is yours

  • A repository in your name

    The site's files, its content and its schema, with every change since the first.

  • All five designs

    The chosen one live, and the others kept.

  • Instructions for your AI

    A CLAUDE.md file written for you, so your AI knows what the site is, how to change it, and what not to touch.

  • A behaviour policy and checks

    A policy for any agent that works on the site, and checks that run before anything goes live.

Everything you receive

Owned, and movable

A design you own is only yours if you can take it with you. The maturity model scores that from level 0, rented, to level 5, sovereign and proven, and a one-minute self-check tells you where your own site stands.

  1. Level 0, rented

    A hosted builder decides everything.

  2. Level 1, exportable

    Your words leave; the design stays.

  3. Level 2, a part you do not control

    Open software, held by one paid plugin, theme or agency component.

  4. Level 3, open, on one host

    Open code, tied to one host's special features.

  5. Level 4, portable

    Plain files, open tools, your domain at your registrar.

  6. Level 5, sovereign and proven

    Portable, and deployed on more than one host.
