Your files, your domain, your host

A website under your own roof.

A website built by agents and kept in a repository you control, at an address in your own name, on any host you choose. Not rented from a platform, and not held by any one company, including us.

Check your website, from 0 to 5

Three rooms, all yours

  • Your files

    The site, its content and its schema, as plain files in a repository with every change since the first. The repository moves into your ownership within three months, sooner if you ask; for now this is a soft requirement.

    What you receive

  • Your domain

    A website you own includes its name, registered to you at a registrar you chose. From sixteen registrars compared, three in the EU were put forward for a European small business: OVHcloud in France, Hostinger in Lithuania and INWX in Germany, each with its catch. A .eu name needs an owner in the EU or EEA.

    Owning your domain

  • Your host

    No server code, no database, no plugin to patch. Any host that can serve files can serve it, without changes: GitHub Pages for free, or any web host, in any region, or your own computer.

    Moving it

Source: The sovereign website, Your own registrar and Static against the platforms.

Owners change, and terms with them

Squarespace was taken private by an investment firm in 2024. A platform's terms, and its jurisdiction, can change with its owners. Your repository does not.

Being able to move is the claim; having moved is the proof. Our sites deploy today to GitHub Pages only. A pipeline that deploys the same commit to many hosts, one of them in Europe and one your own machine, is the next step and does not exist yet. When it does, this site will be its first proof.

Source: Sovereignty you can move, No lock-in and What is a sovereign website.

How far under your roof is your site today?

Sovereignty is not yes or no: it is how far you can move, on your terms. Five questions, one minute.

  1. Level 0, rented

    A hosted builder decides everything.

  2. Level 1, exportable

    Your words leave; the design stays.

  3. Level 2, a part you do not control

    Open software, held by one paid plugin, theme or agency component.

  4. Level 3, open, on one host

    Open code, tied to one host's special features.

  5. Level 4, portable

    Plain files, open tools, your domain at your registrar.

  6. Level 5, sovereign and proven

    Portable, and deployed on more than one host.
