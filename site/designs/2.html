One minute, five questions

Where is your website, from 0 to 5?

Owning a website is the easy part. Moving it is where it fails. Sovereignty is not yes or no: it is how far you can move, on your terms.

  1. 0rented
  2. 1exportable
  3. 2a part you do not control
  4. 3open, on one host
  5. 4portable
  6. 5sovereign and proven
Start with question 1

The self-check

Answer the five questions in order. Stop at your first no: that is your level. If you answer yes to all five, your site is at level 5.

  1. Can you export your words and images?

    • Yes: go on to question 2.
    • No: your site is at level 0, rented.

  2. Can you take the whole site with you, design and functions included, not only the words?

    • Yes: go on to question 3.
    • No: your site is at level 1, exportable.

  3. Does it depend on nothing you do not control: no paid plugin, theme or agency component that holds it where it is?

    • Yes: go on to question 4.
    • No: your site is at level 2, a part you do not control.

  4. Could any host that serves plain files run it without changes, and is its domain registered in your own name, at a registrar you chose?

    Both parts must be yes.

    • Yes: go on to question 5.
    • No: your site is at level 3, open, on one host.

  5. Is it deployed today on more than one host?

    • Yes: your site is at level 5, sovereign and proven.
    • No: your site is at level 4, portable.

Nothing you answer here is sent anywhere: this page has no form, no script and no tracking. The self-check is a guide to the model, not a certificate. What it would take to move up from each level is on the maturity model.

Why one small part is enough

Once there is a piece of proprietary technology in the middle of a site, it does not matter how small it is: you cannot move the site. Nobody else can move it for you either. A paid plugin, a platform's form handler, a hosting company's special function, an agency's private framework: any one of them is enough.

And lock-in hides in more than one place: where the domain is registered, the technology underneath, and the platform it runs on.

The answer we are building: built by agents, owned by you

A team of agents builds your site from what you tell them. You own all of it: the files, the history and the address, in a repository you control. You change it by talking to your own AI, and you can move it anywhere.

  • A team builds it

    An interview about what you want, then a team of agents makes five designs from your own photos and words. You pick one.

  • You own it

    A repository in your name with every change since the first, and, when you have one, your domain in your own name.

  • You change it by talking

    Your own AI changes the site when you ask, inside a behaviour policy that says what it may touch. Every change is a commit, checked before it goes live, and can be undone.

  • You can move it

    Plain files, no server code, no database, no cookies unless you add them. Any host that serves files can run it.

Our own score. This site is at level 4, portable, and says why it is not yet 5: it is deployed to one host, GitHub Pages, and the pipeline that deploys one commit to many hosts does not exist yet. See the examples.
