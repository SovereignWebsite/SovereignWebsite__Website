Where a site is hosted is not sovereignty. Being able to move it is.

Most talk about digital sovereignty is about location: data kept in an EU data centre, a supplier incorporated at home. That matters, and it is not enough. A site hosted in Dublin on a platform whose terms say you cannot take it with you is not yours, wherever the servers are.

Sovereignty is whether you can move your site, its data and its schema to anywhere else, on your own terms. Four tests decide it.

  • Test 1

    Can you move it?

    Yes. Static files in your own repository, with their history, hostable anywhere, from GitHub Pages for free to any web host.

  • Test 2

    Do you own the schema?

    Yes. The structure of the site and its content are files in your repository, in open formats. An export in a format the vendor chose is access, not control.

  • Test 3

    Who sets the terms?

    You. No platform, no subscription, and open-source code that nobody can withdraw.

  • Test 4

    What if we are bought?

    Nothing changes for you. You already hold everything. A company that changes owners can change its terms and its jurisdiction with them; your repository does not.

Source: Sovereignty you can move. Sovereignty here is a set of possibilities, not a guarantee: owning the files means you can move; it does not move them for you.

The five tenets of owning a website

  • 1

    You hold it

    Your own repository, with the full history of every change since the first. Nothing of yours stays with us.

  • 2

    You control it

    No platform, no subscription, no terms but yours, on open code nobody can withdraw.

  • 3

    You change it

    By talking to your own AI, with guard rails. Every change is a commit, and every change can be undone.

  • 4

    You can run it anywhere

    Static files on any host, in any region. No server code, no database, no plugins, no cookies unless you add them.

  • 5

    You keep it

    A site you own should not depend on us existing. If we disappear, you keep it: you already hold everything.

Source: The sovereign website and The pitch.

The difference that matters most: you can leave

The AI is not the difference; everyone has it now. What a platform cannot match without changing its business is that the customer can leave, with their site, whenever they want, and owe nobody anything. In the platforms' own words:

  • Wix: its help page says sites "need to be hosted and operated on Wix's servers".
  • Squarespace: the only export is a WordPress-format file covering pages, one blog and their text and images, leaving out the store, products, audio and video blocks, other page types and the styles.

None of this is hidden; it is the business model. A platform earns every month because you stay. A sovereign agentic website is files in your own repository, which you can take to any host.
