Moving from Wix, Squarespace or WordPress

Stop renting your website.

If you already have a site, you have already answered most of the questions. Your site is the material: the words, the photos, the structure. What changes is who holds it.

What each platform lets you take

The biggest difference between a sovereign website and a platform is not the AI, the designs or the price. It is that you can leave. Here is what the platforms say, in their own words and on their own pages.

  • Wix

    Its help page says sites "need to be hosted and operated on Wix's servers" and cannot be moved to another host. There is no export.

    Moving: your own public pages are still yours to read. An agent can start from them.

  • Squarespace

    The only export is a WordPress-format file with pages, one blog and their text and images. It leaves out the store, products, audio and video blocks, other page types and the styles.

    Moving: that export carries the words and images; the design is made again, in five new directions.

  • WordPress

    WordPress can export everything, as an XML file or through its REST API. Its catch is elsewhere: plugins, themes and a database on a server, which someone has to patch.

    Moving: the export carries pages, posts, images and menus across.

Source: No lock-in and Static against the platforms. The platforms' positions are from their own pages and standard terms, read in November 2026; negotiated contracts may differ. To place your own site, take the self-check.

How a move would work

  1. Start from the site you have

    Give a link to your site, or its export. The interview then asks what you would change, rather than starting from nothing.

  2. Five better versions

    The agents rebuild it as plain files, in five designs made from your old site's words and pictures. You choose one.

  3. Keep your old addresses working

    Redirects from the old addresses to the new pages, so links to your site keep working.

  4. Check it, then switch

    You look at the new site before anything changes. When you are happy, your domain is pointed at it. See owning your domain.

A converter that brings a WordPress site across automatically, and a Squarespace one through its WordPress-format export, is a proposal and does not exist yet. Tools that turn a WordPress site into static files already exist; what this adds is the redesign, the repository in your name, the behaviour policy, and your own AI as the editor afterwards. Source: Static against the platforms and Start with an interview.

Why people leave

  • The rent keeps rising

    This year Squarespace raised three of its four plans by 18% to 26%, and Wix raised its prices on new subscriptions and renewals. When you cannot leave, the price is whatever the platform says.

  • Complexity is attack surface

    Patchstack, which sells WordPress security, counted 11,334 new vulnerabilities in the WordPress ecosystem in 2025, 91% of them in plugins. A static site has no server code, no database and no plugins to patch.

  • Owners change, and terms with them

    Squarespace was taken private by an investment firm in 2024. A platform's terms, and its jurisdiction, can change with its owners. Your repository does not.

Source: Static against the platforms, No lock-in and Sovereignty you can move. The security figures come from a vendor that sells WordPress security; they are the best data there is, and it has an interest. No study measures how much safer static sites are, so we claim no number.

Two honest notes

  • The platforms bundle their editor. Here you change the site with your own Claude, so you need a Claude plan to edit it yourself, or a partner to do it for you.
  • A static site is not automatically safe: your own AI could still publish something private or break a page. That is why every site ships with a behaviour policy, checks and a leak scan before anything goes live.
