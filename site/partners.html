Partners

Partners sell. We build.

The way to market is not to compete with the people who already sell websites, workflows and IT to small businesses. It is to give them what we have: they have the relationships; we have the factory, the agents and the policies.

A first draft. No partner has signed yet, and the terms below are a first draft; the payment terms are a proposal, agreed per partnership. The programme was written for the AgentiCCells website cell and carries across to Sovereign Website. The first partners will shape the rules.

Three kinds of partner

  • 1

    The freelancer

    One person who talks to people and can say: for this amount, I will give you a website. They focus on one kind of customer and want to do more of it.

  • 2

    The small consultancy

    A firm with existing customers who want this, and who might do many: a site for each client, then the workflows behind it.

  • 3

    The larger firm

    Bigger projects, and a reason to want us involved: the orchestration, and people who already know how to run agentic teams.

What a partner sells: a team, not a template

Even the simplest site is a team of agents wired together, not one prompt: a conductor, a researcher, a designer, a developer and a security reviewer, each with a written job description and a behaviour policy, each signing its work. That team is what a partner resells. On a bigger job it grows: a writer, a reviewer, a data agent, a team per workflow.

How a deal works

  1. The partner finds the customer

    And agrees what they want: a site, a flow, a price.

  2. The customer's material comes in

    Photos, videos, words, links; into an encrypted vault if it is private.

  3. The factory sets up the site

    Repository, address, site, CLAUDE.md, behaviour policy.

  4. Five designs

    The customer chooses, with the partner.

  5. Hand-over

    The site is the customer's, in their repository, with their own AI; the partner gets admin access if the customer agrees.

  6. After

    The partner hosts, supports, changes and charges for it as they choose.

The rules of engagement, as drafted

  • The split. When Dinis Cruz is involved, orchestrating and running the factory, the revenue from the sale of the build is split 50/50. Larger projects are agreed case by case.
  • Who invoices. Whoever has the customer. They pay the other their half within 14 days of being paid by the customer: a proposal, agreed in each partnership.
  • Prices. The partner is free to use the published ladder, £500, £1,500, £5,000 and £20,000, or to set their own.
  • Subscriptions are the partner's. Hosting, maintenance, support, changes, a retainer of £50, £100 or £500 a month: no part and no share for us.
  • Who owns what. The customer owns their site, their repository and their material. The partner owns the relationship. The code and policies are open source.
  • Names. Nobody is named publicly, customer or partner, without their agreement.
  • Any country, any language. The agents write in the customer's language, the site serves anywhere, and the partner invoices in their own currency under their own law.

And a partner who reads this, takes the open code and does it alone is still a win: one more team running the technology, and one more place where agent behaviour policies are used for real. The open pattern is free.
